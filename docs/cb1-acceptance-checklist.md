# Craftbox CB1: manual acceptance checklist (about 25 minutes)

Setup: run `start-craftbox.bat`. To keep your real history clean, first `set CRAFTBOX_DATA_DIR=%TEMP%\craftbox-cb1-manual`. Start in the **Story Lab** tab (the default) for section 0, then use the **Scene Lab** tab. Dreambox on port 8000 may stay running. Tick each box; write down anything that surprised you.

## 0. Story Lab: the six-phase story process (about 10 minutes)
- [ ] The Story Lab opens first with "Step 1 of 6: audit the concept"; the stepper shows Concept, Hook, Empathy, Conflict, Structure, Revision.
- [ ] Start a story (title, protagonist, antagonist) and press **Create the project**. The five pillars appear with protagonist and antagonist filled in.
- [ ] Press **Check my answers**: items are marked answered (tick), advice (!) or not answered (circle). Fill in the core conflict and the three minimal-narrative boxes and check again: they turn to ticks. Make the two events identical: advice, not an error.
- [ ] Choose a plot shape. **Save story revision**: "Saved", and everything stays on screen.
- [ ] Load the sample story instead. Phase 2: the hook is delivered by a line at about 4 s ("inside the first 15 s"). Change the hook line to the last line and check: advice that it lands too late.
- [ ] Phase 3: clear the cost of one choice and rate the distress risk "high": both give advice, and Save still works (advice never blocks).
- [ ] Phase 4: add an expectation gap, an information gap and a layer of subtext, linked to beats and script lines.
- [ ] Phase 5: all six told-story parts and the eight story-circle steps are filled. Link the first circle step to the last beat: advice that the journey goes backwards.
- [ ] Phase 6: the first-draft line count is shown; add a reader's note, tick a polish item, tick the flight-simulator verification: the checks update.
- [ ] The last step has no Next, only **Continue to the Scene Lab** and **Review phase 1 again**. Continue: you land on step 2 of the Scene Lab with the same project, and step 1 there shows "Story audit … answered well".
- [ ] Reload (F5) on any tab: you return to the same tab, step and unsaved answers.
- [ ] Does each phase's explanation make sense, and do the questions help you find holes in your own story? (the main judgement)

## A. First impressions (read it cold)
- [ ] Every step (of both labs) has "Step N of 8" or "Step N of 6", a plain-words paragraph saying what the step is for, and Back / Next buttons that say where they lead.
- [ ] Any greyed-out button shows a yellow reason beside it (try step 1 Next with nothing loaded).
- [ ] The explanations make sense to someone who does not know the software.

## B. Story idea to screenplay (steps 1-2)
- [ ] Fill the story idea (title, one-line story, location, two characters) and press **Create the screenplay outline**: a notice appears, Next is enabled.
- [ ] In step 2, the **Characters** tab lists both people with permanent IDs (C-001, C-002); names keep the capitalisation you typed.
- [ ] Add a few lines (action, dialogue). Each gets an ID like SC-001-E01; reorder with the arrows; delete one.
- [ ] Press **Save screenplay revision**: "Saved", and what you typed is still on screen. Change one word: "Changed, not saved yet".
- [ ] Go back to step 1 and press **Load the sample scene**: because you have unsaved work you are asked before it is replaced. Choose **Keep working**.
- [ ] Reload the page (F5): you return to the same step with your text.

## C. The sample scene "The Closing Hour" (steps 2-4)
- [ ] Load the sample, step 2 → **Script** shows 24 lines. Read it: does the emotional progression work as a scene (cold return of the book → small cuts → the ticket → "Ask me" → the sign)?
- [ ] Save revision 1 with a note. Edit line E12, save revision 2. Open **Revision history**: two rows. Pick revision 1 and 2 in **Compare** and **Show differences**: the changed line is named.
- [ ] **Open this revision** on revision 1: it loads; saving later continues from it as a new revision (nothing replaced).
- [ ] Step 3: **Recalculate estimates** shows a length between 60 and 90 s, frames at 24 fps and a pacing label. Set **Fix the length** to 70 and recalculate: 70 s.
- [ ] Step 3: add a scene, move it up and down; its ID does not change. Add a sequence.
- [ ] Step 4: seven beats with an intensity curve rising to 10 then falling; move a slider and the number changes. **Spread the script lines across the beats** assigns lines evenly. A line can only belong to one beat.
- [ ] **Import / export** tab: **Download JSON**, **Show readable script** (headings like `INT. PAGE & CANDLE BOOKSHOP - NIGHT #SC-001#`), then paste a short script of your own into **Import** (you are asked before replacing unsaved work).

## D. Blocking (step 5)
- [ ] Next is disabled until the screenplay is saved, with the reason shown.
- [ ] Choose **A, Intimate** and **Draft the plan**: a notice reports the number of shots and total seconds.
- [ ] Read "How the director reads the scene": does the intent describe the same scene? Edit a sentence.
- [ ] Pick the beat **Open it** then **Ask me**: the two characters are closer in the later beat. The stage diagram shows start (hollow), end (filled), path and where each looks.
- [ ] **Move by clicking the stage** for one character, click the stage: their end position changes. Change **Looks** for one character; the short line on the diagram turns.
- [ ] **Save direction plan version**: "Saved a new plan version"; the plan list at the bottom shows both versions.

## E. Shots (step 6)
- [ ] The table lists the shots with size, framing, angle, movement, side, length and **purpose**. Does the sequence vary (not the same framing again and again)? Is the opening an establishing shot and the end a closing shot?
- [ ] Click a shot number: its editor shows **Why this shot** and a note in your words that names the beat and quotes the line it covers.
- [ ] Reorder one shot (↑ ↓), **Reject** one, **Approve** another, **Copy** one. The rejected row is struck through.
- [ ] **Add a shot** and press Save without a note: an error appears beside the Save button naming `purpose_note`, and your edits are still there. Fill the note, save.
- [ ] **Check this plan**: "No problems found". Now set the side of one middle shot to **B** and check again: an error mentions the **180° line** and the shot row turns red. Tick **motivated crossing of the line**: that error clears (the cut back is still flagged).
- [ ] Set every shot to the same size and check: a variety warning appears.

## F. Two approaches (steps 5 and 7)
- [ ] Back in step 5 choose **B, Observational** and **Draft another plan**. Compare in step 7 ("Compare with another approach"): shots, average length, close-ups, camera moves and camera sides differ, and the two statements of intent read differently.
- [ ] Which approach would you actually shoot? Is each one a believable, deliberate reading of the same script? (This is the main creative judgement.)

## G. Director review and approval (step 7)
- [ ] Metrics, the pacing table (shorter shots where intensity is higher) and the problems list are shown.
- [ ] **Approve all undecided shot(s)**, save, then **Submit for review**. **Approve the plan** without a name: error beside the button. Enter your name and approve: status **approved**; your name and note stay in the boxes.

## H. Script changes after approval (the integrity check)
- [ ] With an approved plan, go to step 2 (use the step bar), change the line "The day after you left…", and save a new revision.
- [ ] Return to step 7: a yellow panel says the script changed and lists the dependent shots (a few shots in the "What he kept" beat). The earlier approved plan still says **approved**.
- [ ] Step 8: **Download** the scene manifest: it is refused with a clear reason and an **Export anyway (recorded)** button appears.
- [ ] Back in step 7 press **Rebase onto the latest screenplay**: a new plan version appears; only the listed shots are "needs review", the rest stay approved. The old version is still in the list, unchanged.

## I. Export (step 8)
- [ ] Without approval the two manifests are disabled with a reason; the other files download any time.
- [ ] With an approved plan and decided shots: download **Scene manifest** and **Shot manifest**. Open them: `auto_import` is false, your name is under approval, shots have start frame and duration, previz and storyboard fields are present, rejected shots are listed as excluded.
- [ ] Download the same manifest twice: identical content.
- [ ] Download the **Shot plan for Cinematography / Editing**, then in the **Single craft** tab run Editing with `{"shot_plan": <the file>}`: it works.
- [ ] If any shot is undecided, the shot manifest asks for **Export anyway (recorded)** and the file records `allow_pending`.

## J. History and compatibility
- [ ] **History** lists the screenplay revisions and plan versions; select two runs of the same craft and compare.
- [ ] **Single craft** tab: Screenwriting scene card, Cinematography and Editing still run as before; Lighting (or any planned craft) still cannot run; Direction now can.
- [ ] Stop Craftbox (Ctrl+C) and start it again: revisions, plans, approvals and exports are all still there.
- [ ] `samples\README.md` lists the generated sample files; open a couple.

## Not in CB1 (so do not expect it)
AI-written text, video or image generation, Dreambox import, constraint policies, drag-and-drop staging, multi-character line-of-action modelling.
